=====================================================================

---------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------------------------

This report summarizes every transformer-based model that is currently running
in production, in limited rollout, or under evaluation across NovaMind Labs
products. It covers why each model was chosen, how it is deployed, how it
performs, what it costs, which incidents it caused, and what we plan to do next.

Key numbers for the reporting period:

- Total transformer models in production: 7
- Models in limited rollout (canary or beta): 2
- Models under evaluation: 4
- Total monthly inference requests: about 80 million (plus 9,000 hours of audio)
- Total monthly inference cost: about 61,400 USD
- Overall p95 latency across all online endpoints: 241 ms (SLO is 300 ms)
- Uptime across all model endpoints: 99.93%
- Number of Sev-1 incidents: 1, number of Sev-2 incidents: 4

Top three wins of the period:

1. Replacing MobileViT with EfficientFormer cut on-device image classification
   latency by 18% and reduced app crash reports related to the camera screen by 31%.
2. Moving the support-chat assistant from LLaMA 2 13B to Mistral 7B with
   AWQ 4-bit quantization reduced GPU cost for that service by 44%.
3. Adding a reranker (cross-encoder MiniLM) to the RAG pipeline increased
   answer accuracy on our internal QA benchmark from 71% to 83%.

Top three risks:

1. LLaMA 2 licensing review is still open for one enterprise customer contract.
2. The Reformer experiment on embedded devices has unstable training.
3. GPU capacity in the us-east-1 region is close to its limit during peak hours.

SECTION 2: TRANSFORMER VARIANTS IN PRODUCTION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 EfficientFormer (Vision, on-device)
---------------------------------------

Use case:
   - On-device image classification in the NovaMind mobile app
     (product recognition from the camera screen).

Why it was chosen:
   - Very low latency: under 2 ms per image on the iPhone 12 Neural Engine.
   - Small size: EfficientFormer-L1 is about 12 million parameters.
   - Pure transformer design that still runs well on mobile hardware.

Deployment details:
   - Converted to Core ML for iOS and to ONNX, then TensorFlow Lite, for Android.
   - Model file size after FP16 conversion: 24 MB.
   - Shipped inside the app bundle, updated through app releases every 4 weeks.

Results:
   - Replaced MobileViT after an A/B test with 200,000 users over 3 weeks.
   - Inference was 18% faster than MobileViT.
   - Top-1 accuracy on our product dataset: 91.4% (MobileViT was 90.8%).
   - Camera-screen crash reports dropped by 31% because of lower memory use.

Known issues:
   - On older Android devices (less than 3 GB RAM), first-run latency is up to 40 ms
     because of model warm-up.
   - Mitigation: model is now preloaded when the app starts.


2.2 DistilBERT (NLP, semantic search)
-------------------------------------

Use case:
   - Semantic search over internal support tickets and the help-center knowledge base.

Why it was chosen:
   - 40% smaller than BERT-base while keeping about 97% of its accuracy on our benchmark.
   - 60% faster inference than BERT-base on CPU.

Deployment details:
   - Served behind a FastAPI microservice.
   - Redis cache in front of the service; cache hit rate is about 38%.
   - Runs on CPU-only AWS Fargate tasks with autoscaling between 4 and 20 tasks.
   - Embeddings are 768-dimensional and stored in a FAISS IVF index.

Results:
   - p95 latency: 85 ms.
   - Monthly requests: about 38 million.
   - Monthly cost: about 2,900 USD.
   - Recall@10 on the internal ticket search benchmark: 0.86.

Status and plan:
   - Being phased out for intent classification tasks in favour of TinyBERT
     because of memory limits on the edge gateways.
   - Will stay in use for semantic search until the BGE-small migration (see Section 6).


2.3 TinyBERT (NLP, email triage)
--------------------------------

Use case:
   - Classification of incoming emails into 14 categories (billing, bug report,
     feature request, legal, spam, and others) for the internal email triage system.

Deployment details:
   - Fine-tuned TinyBERT-4L (about 14.5 million parameters).
   - Integrated into the Outlook plugin through TensorFlow Lite, so it runs on the
     employee's machine and email text never leaves the device.
   - A server-side copy also runs on Fargate for the shared support inbox.

Results:
   - Macro F1 score: 0.89 across the 14 categories.
   - Average inference time on a laptop CPU: 9 ms.
   - Saves the support team about 120 hours per month of manual triage.

Known issues:
   - Accuracy drops to 0.74 F1 on emails that mix English and Hindi.
   - Planned fix: fine-tune a multilingual model (see Section 6).


2.4 LLaMA 2 13B (LLM, internal chat assistant)
----------------------------------------------

Use case:
   - Base model for "Nova Assist", the internal employee chat assistant used by
     engineering, sales and HR.

Deployment details:
   - LLaMA 2 13B with a proprietary instruction-tuned layer trained using LoRA adapters.
   - Combined with domain-specific knowledge graphs (product catalog, org chart,
     internal policies) stored in Neo4j.
   - Prompt format adapted for multi-turn chat memory with a RAG backend.
   - Orchestration experiments were done with both LlamaIndex and LangGraph.
     LangGraph was chosen for production because it gave explicit control over
     multi-step flows and retries.
   - Served with vLLM on 2 x A10G GPUs per replica, 3 replicas.

Results:
   - Average answer latency: 1.9 seconds for the first token, 6.5 seconds for a full answer.
   - Weekly active users: about 2,300 employees.
   - Thumbs-up rate on answers: 78%.
   - Monthly cost: about 14,800 USD.

Status and plan:
   - Licensing review is still open for one enterprise customer who wants
     Nova Assist inside their own tenant.
   - Evaluation of Mistral 7B and LLaMA 3 8B as replacements is in progress.


2.5 Mistral 7B Instruct (LLM, customer support chat)
-----------------------------------------------------

Use case:
   - Customer-facing support chatbot on the NovaMind website and inside the app.

Why it was chosen:
   - Similar quality to LLaMA 2 13B on our support benchmark (score 7.6 vs 7.8 out of 10)
     at about half the size.
   - Sliding window attention and grouped-query attention give faster inference.
   - Apache 2.0 license, which made the legal review simple.

Deployment details:
   - Quantized with AWQ to 4-bit.
   - Served with vLLM on single A10G GPUs, autoscaling between 2 and 8 replicas.
   - Uses the shared RAG pipeline described in Section 4.

Results:
   - GPU cost for the support-chat service went down by 44% after migration.
   - First-token latency: 0.7 seconds; full answer: 3.1 seconds on average.
   - Ticket deflection rate (questions solved without a human agent): 46%.
   - Monthly conversations: about 520,000.
   - Monthly cost: about 9,600 USD.


2.6 Whisper Small (Speech, meeting transcription)
--------------------------------------------------

Use case:
   - Transcription of internal meetings and customer calls (with consent).

Deployment details:
   - Whisper Small (244 million parameters) running with faster-whisper (CTranslate2)
     on GPU, INT8 precision.
   - Batch jobs run every 15 minutes on spot GPU instances.

Results:
   - Word error rate (WER) on English calls: 8.2%.
   - WER on Indian-English calls: 11.9%.
   - Processes about 9,000 hours of audio per month.
   - Monthly cost: about 3,100 USD (spot instances save around 60% versus on-demand).

Notes:
   - Wav2Vec 2.0 was benchmarked earlier for voice assistant integrations,
     but Whisper gave better accuracy on noisy audio, so Wav2Vec 2.0 was not adopted.


2.7 MiniLM Cross-Encoder (Reranking)
------------------------------------

Use case:
   - Reranks the top 20 chunks retrieved from the vector store before they are
     sent to the LLM in every RAG flow.

Deployment details:
   - cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2, served on Triton with dynamic batching.
   - Runs on CPU; average 22 ms for 20 query-chunk pairs.

Results:
   - Internal QA benchmark accuracy went from 71% to 83% after adding the reranker.
   - Hallucination rate (answers that cite content not in the documents) went
     from 9% to 4%.

SECTION 3: MODELS IN LIMITED ROLLOUT AND UNDER EVALUATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Limited rollout

Swin Transformer (Vision, manufacturing quality control):
   - Running on 2 of our 6 partner factory lines as a canary.
   - Detects surface defects on metal parts from high-resolution camera images.
   - Defect recall: 94%, false positive rate: 3.5%.
   - Compared with ViT-B/16, Swin gave 6% higher recall on small defects because of
     its hierarchical windows.
   - Edge deployment on NVIDIA Jetson Orin with TensorRT, 28 ms per image.

LLaMA 3 8B Instruct (LLM, internal beta):
   - 10% of Nova Assist traffic is routed to LLaMA 3 8B.
   - Early results: thumbs-up rate 81% (vs 78% for LLaMA 2 13B) and 35% lower cost.
   - Expected to fully replace LLaMA 2 13B by end of Q4 2025 if licensing is cleared.

3.2 Under evaluation

Reformer:
   - Tested for memory efficiency on embedded devices for long sensor logs.
   - LSH (locality-sensitive hashing) attention gave a 60% memory reduction.
   - Integration challenges with standard transformer tooling because of bucket
     collisions in LSH attention.
   - Training is unstable; loss spikes appear after about 30,000 steps.
   - Ongoing investigation of gradient clipping (max norm 0.5 vs 1.0) and
     learning-rate warm-up strategies.

ViT (Vision Transformer):
   - Evaluated for manufacturing QC before Swin was chosen.
   - Needs large training data; with our 40,000 labelled images it overfit.

Longformer:
   - Evaluated for summarizing long legal contracts (up to 16,000 tokens).
   - Good quality, but LLM-based summarization with chunking was simpler to maintain.

Phi-3 Mini:
   - Evaluated as an on-device LLM for offline help inside the mobile app.
   - 3.8 billion parameters; runs at about 12 tokens per second on flagship phones.
   - Battery drain is the main concern; decision expected in Q1 2026.

SECTION 4: RAG PIPELINE ARCHITECTURE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

All LLM-based products share one RAG pipeline, codename "Atlas".

Ingestion:
   - Sources: Confluence, Google Drive, Jira, Zendesk help center, product PDFs.
   - Documents are pulled every 6 hours.
   - HTML is cleaned and text is split with a recursive character splitter:
     chunk size 500 characters, overlap 50 characters.
   - About 1.8 million chunks are indexed in total.

Embeddings:
   - Current model: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384 dimensions) for general documents.
   - DistilBERT embeddings are still used for the support-ticket index.

Vector store:
   - FAISS indexes, rebuilt nightly from the document store.
   - HNSW index for the general index, IVF index for the ticket index.

Retrieval flow:
   1. Query rewriting with the LLM (expands abbreviations and product codes).
   2. Hybrid retrieval: BM25 keyword search + vector search, top 20 results merged.
   3. Reranking with the MiniLM cross-encoder, top 5 kept.
   4. Answer generation with citations to the source chunks.

Multi-agent orchestration:
   - Complex requests in Nova Assist use a LangGraph supervisor with three agents:
     a research agent (internal search + web search), a math/analytics agent
     (SQL and calculations), and a writer agent (reports and summaries).
   - The supervisor routes one task at a time and stops after at most 12 steps.
   - Average multi-agent request takes 14 seconds and costs about 4 times a normal request.

SECTION 5: DEPLOYMENT ARCHITECTURE AND INFRASTRUCTURE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Serving:
   - Most models are served through NVIDIA Triton Inference Server with dynamic batching.
   - LLMs (LLaMA 2, LLaMA 3, Mistral) are served with vLLM for paged attention and
     continuous batching.
   - GPU inference runs on AWS EKS node groups with A10G instances (g5.xlarge and g5.2xlarge).
   - CPU-only models (TinyBERT, DistilBERT, MiniLM reranker) run on autoscaled Fargate tasks.

Regions:
   - Primary: us-east-1. Secondary: eu-west-1 for European customers (GDPR).
   - us-east-1 GPU usage reaches 92% during peak hours (14:00-18:00 UTC).

Monitoring:
   - Prometheus and Grafana for metrics; latency SLO is p95 under 300 ms for
     non-LLM endpoints and first token under 1.5 seconds for LLM endpoints.
   - LangSmith for tracing LLM and agent calls.
   - Data drift checks run weekly with Evidently AI.

CI/CD:
   - GitHub Actions -> Docker -> EKS with Helm charts.
   - Every model update goes through a canary rollout: 5% traffic for 24 hours,
     then 25%, then 100%, with automatic rollback if error rate rises above 1%.

Experiment tracking:
   - DVC for dataset versioning, MLflow for experiments and the model registry.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6: OPTIMIZATIONS AND TECHNIQUES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Training:
   - Knowledge distillation from teacher to student models using response alignment
     (used for TinyBERT and DistilBERT fine-tuning).
   - Knowledge injection through adapters (LoRA rank 16) instead of full fine-tuning.
   - Mixed-precision training (AMP, BF16) on NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

Inference:
   - FlashAttention-2 gave a 2.1x speed-up on long prompts (above 4,000 tokens).
   - Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE) scaling allowed LLaMA-based models to handle
     8,000-token contexts.
   - AWQ 4-bit quantization for Mistral 7B: 3.4x less GPU memory with less than
     1 point drop on our benchmark.
   - INT8 quantization with TensorRT for vision models on the edge.
   - KV-cache reuse for repeated system prompts saves about 18% of GPU time in chat.

Tokenizers:
   - SentencePiece, BPE and unigram tokenizers were compared for multilingual tasks.
   - SentencePiece unigram gave the best results on Hindi and Tamil text, with
     about 22% fewer tokens per sentence than BPE.

Planned work (Q4 2025 - Q1 2026):
   - Migrate the support-ticket index from DistilBERT to BGE-small embeddings.
   - Fine-tune multilingual-MiniLM for Hindi-English email triage.
   - Fully replace LLaMA 2 13B with LLaMA 3 8B.
   - Add a GPU node group in us-west-2 to reduce peak pressure in us-east-1.

SECTION 8: INCIDENTS AND LESSONS LEARNED
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Incident 1 (Sev-1, March 2025): Support chatbot gave outdated refund policy
   - Cause: the FAISS index rebuild failed silently for 3 nights, so the chatbot used
     an old version of the refund policy document.
   - Impact: about 1,400 customers received wrong refund information.
   - Fix: index rebuild jobs now alert on failure, and every answer shows the
     "last updated" date of its source document.
   - Lesson: freshness of the knowledge base is as important as model quality.

Incident 2 (Sev-2, April 2025): GPU out-of-memory in Nova Assist
   - Cause: very long chat histories (over 30 turns) exceeded the KV-cache budget.
   - Fix: chat memory is now summarized after 10 turns.

Incident 3 (Sev-2, May 2025): Multi-agent loop
   - Cause: the research agent and writer agent kept handing the task back to each
     other because neither produced a final answer.
   - Impact: some requests ran for over 2 minutes and cost 20 times a normal request.
   - Fix: a hard step limit (recursion limit of 12) and a "FINAL ANSWER" stop marker.

Incident 4 (Sev-2, June 2025): Swin false positives after lighting change
   - Cause: a factory replaced its lights, which changed image colors.
   - Fix: added color-jitter augmentation and a weekly drift check on image statistics.

Incident 5 (Sev-2, August 2025): Whisper transcription delays
   - Cause: spot GPU instances were reclaimed during a regional capacity shortage.
   - Fix: fallback to on-demand instances when the job queue is older than 1 hour.

General lessons:
   - Smaller, well-tuned models (Mistral 7B, TinyBERT) often beat larger models on
     cost per useful answer.
   - A reranker is one of the cheapest ways to improve RAG answer quality.
   - Multi-agent systems need step limits, cost tracking and clear stop conditions.
   - Every model change must go through canary rollout, even "small" ones.

SECTION 9: GLOSSARY
---------------------------------------------------------------------

   AWQ           Activation-aware Weight Quantization, a 4-bit quantization method.
   Canary        Releasing a change to a small share of traffic before everyone.
   FAISS         Facebook AI Similarity Search, a library for vector search.
   FlashAttention  A faster, memory-efficient way to compute attention on GPUs.
   LoRA          Low-Rank Adaptation, a cheap fine-tuning method using small adapters.
   LSH attention Attention that groups similar tokens with hashing (used in Reformer).
   p95 latency   95% of requests are faster than this value.
   RAG           Retrieval-Augmented Generation.
   Reranker      A model that re-sorts retrieved chunks by relevance to the query.
   RoPE          Rotary Positional Encoding, a way to encode token positions.
   vLLM          An open-source LLM serving engine with paged attention.
   WER           Word Error Rate, the share of words a speech model gets wrong.
